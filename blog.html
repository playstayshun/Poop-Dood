Health & Safety

Why Dog Poop Is More Dangerous Than You Think

🗓 April 21, 2026 ✍️ Corbin Key, PoopDood ⏱ 4 min read

Most dog owners think of poop cleanup as a chore — something you do so your yard looks nice and smells okay. But here's the thing: dog waste is actually classified as a non-point source pollutant by the EPA, putting it in the same category as oil spills and toxic chemicals when it comes to environmental impact. That's not exaggerating. That's the actual regulation.

A single gram of dog poop contains an estimated 23 million fecal coliform bacteria. That's bacteria like E. coli and salmonella — the kind that can make people seriously sick, especially children who play in the yard or adults with weakened immune systems.

What's actually in dog poop?

  • E. coli — can cause severe stomach illness in humans
  • Salmonella — spreads to humans through soil and water contact
  • Giardia — a parasite that causes intestinal infections
  • Campylobacter — a leading cause of food poisoning in the US
  • Roundworms and hookworms — can survive in soil for years
  • Parvovirus — deadly to unvaccinated dogs
💡 Roundworm and hookworm eggs can remain alive and infectious in your soil for up to 4 years after the waste is gone. Simply hosing down the yard doesn't eliminate them — physical removal is the only reliable solution.

It doesn't just stay in your yard

When it rains, dog waste runoff flows into storm drains and eventually into local waterways. In Lubbock, that means it can end up affecting local playa lakes and drainage systems. This is one of the main reasons cities across the US have leash laws and poop-pickup ordinances — it's a genuine public health issue, not just a neighborhood etiquette thing.

What about dog poop as fertilizer?

You might have heard that dog poop is "natural" and can fertilize your lawn like cow manure. This is a common misconception. Cows are herbivores — their waste is lower in nitrogen and pathogens. Dogs are carnivores. Their waste is high in nitrogen (causing those brown burn patches on your grass) and loaded with the pathogens listed above. Leaving it on your lawn actively damages your grass and contaminates the soil.

The bottom line: regular, consistent dog waste removal is one of the simplest things you can do to protect your family, your pets, your yard, and your neighborhood. That's exactly why PoopDood exists.

How Often Should You Clean Up Dog Poop in Your Yard?

🗓 May 5, 2026 ✍️ Corbin Key, PoopDood ⏱ 3 min read

One of the most common questions we get at PoopDood is: "How often should I actually be cleaning up after my dog?" The honest answer is — more often than most people do. But the right frequency really does depend on a few factors specific to your situation.

The general rule: at least twice a week

For a single dog in an average-sized backyard, cleaning up at least twice a week keeps odors manageable, prevents bacteria from building up in the soil, and stops flies from breeding. If you can do it every day, even better — but twice a week is the realistic minimum for most households.

Multiple dogs? You need to go more often.

  • 1 dog — twice a week is fine, weekly works if the yard is large
  • 2 dogs — aim for 2–3 times per week
  • 3–4 dogs — weekly professional service is strongly recommended
  • 5+ dogs — twice-weekly service keeps things under control

Season matters too

In Lubbock summers, heat accelerates bacterial growth and makes odors much worse. Waste that sat fine for a week in February can become a serious problem within a day or two in July. During hot months, we recommend bumping up your cleanup frequency or switching to a more frequent service plan.

💡 Here's a simple test: if you can smell your yard from the porch, or if you can spot more than 5–6 piles when scanning your lawn, it's been too long since the last cleanup. Time to call PoopDood.

What happens when you let it go too long?

Beyond the obvious smell, infrequent cleanup leads to nitrogen burn (those dead brown patches in your grass), increased fly and pest activity, and a much harder cleanup job when you finally do get to it. Old, dried waste is significantly harder to remove than fresh waste — and parasites like roundworm eggs become more deeply embedded in the soil over time.

If life gets busy and the yard gets away from you, that's what one-time cleanups are for. We show up, clear everything out, and get you back to a clean slate. Then you decide if you want to keep up with a recurring plan.

5 Signs Your Yard Needs a Professional Pooper Scooper

🗓 May 19, 2026 ✍️ Corbin Key, PoopDood ⏱ 3 min read

Most people wait too long before calling a professional pooper scooper. It's understandable — it feels like something you should just handle yourself. But there are some clear signs that the situation has gotten past the point of a quick DIY fix, or that your schedule just doesn't allow for it anymore.

1. You can smell the yard from inside

If you open a back window or walk out onto your porch and you're hit with a smell, your yard has accumulated enough waste that the ammonia and bacteria are off-gassing in a real way. At this point you're not just dealing with cosmetics — you've got an active odor and hygiene problem.

2. Your grass has dead brown patches

Dog waste contains high concentrations of nitrogen. In small amounts, nitrogen is good for grass. In concentrated amounts from repeated waste in the same spots, it chemically burns the lawn. Those dead patches are a classic sign of accumulated waste — and they won't recover until the waste is properly removed and the soil gets time to reset.

3. Flies are multiplying in your yard

Dog waste is one of the top breeding grounds for flies. If you've noticed a sudden increase in flies around your patio or back door, there's a good chance poop buildup in the yard is the source. This also creates problems for neighbors nearby.

4. You've avoided the yard for more than a week

This one's simple but honest: if you or your family have stopped using the backyard because of the state of it, that's the sign right there. Your yard should be a space you enjoy, not a minefield you avoid.

5. Cleanup keeps getting postponed

Life gets busy. Kids, work, weekends that fill up fast — it happens to everyone. If you've told yourself "I'll get to it this weekend" for three weekends in a row, a recurring service plan is the practical solution. It removes the task from your to-do list entirely so you never have to think about it.

The Best Dog Parks in Lubbock, TX (And What to Bring)

🗓 May 26, 2026 ✍️ Corbin Key, PoopDood ⏱ 5 min read

Lubbock is a great city for dog owners. Wide open spaces, mild winters (mostly), and a community that genuinely loves its pets. If you're looking for places to give your dog some off-leash freedom or just a good walk, here's a rundown of the top dog-friendly spots in town — plus what you should always have with you.

Top dog-friendly spots in Lubbock

  • McAlister Dog Park (Pictured) — One of the most popular spots in town, with 5 acres of space. With plenty of room to roam it tends to be busier on weekends, so arrive early/evening for a calmer experience.
  • Clapp Park — A solid neighborhood park with good walking paths and a friendly atmosphere. Popular with families and dogs alike.
  • Yellowhouse Canyon Lakes — A beautiful natural area on the east side. The trails along the canyon are great for longer walks and the scenery is genuinely impressive for West Texas.
  • Lubbock Lake Landmark — Unique historical site with walking paths. Good for a different kind of outing with your dog.
  • Raintree Park — Quieter neighborhood park, great for quick evening walks and less crowded than the bigger parks.
💡 Lubbock has leash laws in public parks — dogs must be leashed unless in a designated off-leash area. Always check the park's current signage before letting your dog run free.

What to always bring to a Lubbock dog park

  • Waste bags (at least 3 — always bring more than you think you'll need)
  • A water bowl and fresh water, especially May through September
  • A leash even if you plan to go off-leash — situations change fast
  • Proof of vaccinations if going to a formal dog park area
  • Flea and tick protection — West Texas has both, especially after rain
  • Your phone with PoopDood's number saved — 806-781-5638 😄

A note on poop bag etiquette

This one's personal for us, obviously. Lubbock parks have waste stations with free bags at most locations, but they run out. Always carry your own. And if you see a bag left on the ground (people sometimes bag it and forget to throw it away), do a neighbor a favor and toss it in the nearest bin. It takes 5 seconds and makes the park better for everyone.

The best thing about being a responsible dog owner in Lubbock is that the community genuinely notices and appreciates it. Clean parks, clean yards, happy dogs. That's the goal.
